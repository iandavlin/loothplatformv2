A design for it — and two things I found on live while measuring it, one of which means the page nobody can reach is the one we ask people to sign up on.

Two live defects, found while scoping this

1. The signup page redirects anonymous visitors to a login screen

Measured on live, from the live box (never a public curl — Cloudflare turns that into a misleading 403):

URL, requested anonymouslyResult
/weekly-email-sign-up/ 302 → wp-login.php?…&action=bpnoaccess
/looth-group-weekly/ (the archive, next door) 200 OK

The cause is a hand-maintained allow-list with 67 entries — bp-enable-private-network-public-content. /looth-group-weekly/ is in it. /weekly-email-sign-up/ is not. The archive got allow-listed and the signup page beside it was missed.

Why this matters more than it looks: this page's audience is non-members — 188 of the 204 confirmed subscribers on that list have no user account at all. A login wall is not an inconvenience for them, it is the whole door.

The fix is one line in that setting, and it is a live admin change, so it is yours to make, not mine. I have not touched it.

2. Signing up would soon subscribe people to silence

Everyone who signs up here lands on list 7, Non Member Weekly Email Subscriber. They have no account, so they can never have a to-do item — and the “empty means send no email” ruling drops anyone with nothing waiting. As my branch stands, all 195 of them would silently stop receiving the digest.

My recommendation is that the ruling should govern the recap SECTION's audience, not the whole email's — non-members keep getting the events, videos and loothprint, with no recap section. That is exactly what they signed up for and exactly what they received on 27 July. It narrows your ruling, so I have not built it. Full detail: RECAP-SUPPRESSION-PROPOSAL.md §5.1.

What the design is doing, and why each part is there rather than decorative:

ElementWhy
Cadence stated up front — “once a week, Mondays, ~4 min” The single most common reason people don't subscribe is not knowing how often it will arrive. The current page says nothing.
“One more step: we'll email you a confirmation link” Form 5 has double_opt_in: true, so that email is unavoidable. Measured: 9 of 128 signups in the last 12 months are still pending (7%) — they signed up and never confirmed. Setting the expectation is the cheapest fix available.
Email + consent checkbox, and nothing else That is form 5's actual field set (email + gdpr-agreement). The mock deliberately invents no fields, so the build is a port rather than a redesign.
A real issue embedded, scrollable Rendered by the same builder that sends it — not an illustration. Showing the thing beats describing it.
“Already a member?” panel at the bottom Members are already on this list via membership. Without this they sign up twice and land in two lists, which is how list 3 and list 7 diverged in the first place.
No section for choosing topics or frequency There is one digest and one cadence. A picker would promise control that does not exist.

Brand tokens are the email builder's own (gold #ECB351, dark #2B2318, mint #87986A, paper #FAF6EE) so the page and the email it advertises look like one thing. The mock is self-contained HTML, responsive at 880px and 520px, and the sample email is the only iframe.

3. The allow-list line — /weekly-email-sign-up/ needs adding to bp-enable-private-network-public-content. That is a live admin setting. Worth doing whether or not you like the mock, because the page is unreachable today.
