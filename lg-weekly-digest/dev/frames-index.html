The three decisions

Everything else I can call myself. These three change the design, so they are drawn below rather than argued. Frames A–D under “Full week” show each one against the same real data.

1. May discussion titles appear? A is my recommendation

A — name the discussion. “2 new replies on your discussion — “Suggest an alternative to concave fret file” — Doug Proper and 1 other.”

B — counts and senders only. “2 new replies on your discussion — Doug Proper and 1 other.”

Why A: the title is what makes the row worth the tap, and a public forum title is already public — it is on the Hub, in search, in the digest’s own “From the Forum” section two inches below. It is not message content, which is what the ruling forbids. The catch: a private-forum or tier-gated thread title is not public, and the recap has no idea which is which today — so if you pick A, I gate titles on forums.forum.visibility = 'public' and fall back to B’s wording for anything else. The thread-follow lane is asking you this same question (its §6 Q3); one answer settles both.

2. Per-row deep links, or one “Open the Hub” button? A is my recommendation

A — every row links to its own target (frame A/B): the mention opens that reply in the discussion modal, the reaction opens that post, the DM opens the sender’s profile.

C — rows are inert, one gold “Open the Hub →” button under the list (frame C).

Why A: your brief asked for deep links, and the links already exist — the notification bridge stamped a canonical /hub/?topic=…&reply=… on every row when the event fired. We are reusing them, not building a second link system. Worth knowing: the thread-follow lane’s spec (§2.6b) proposes exactly the opposite — one Hub link, “keeps the email inert.” You have two lanes pointed in different directions on this one; A follows your brief to me.

3. Top or bottom? A is my recommendation

A — directly under the intro rule, above the curated content (frames A/B/C).

D — below the curated content, just above the sign-off (frame D).

Why A: it is the only part of the email that is about them, and it is three or four lines. Putting it first costs the curated content almost no attention and gives the whole email a reason to be opened by someone who does not read newsletters. D is defensible if you consider the digest primarily an editorial product and the recap a footer service.

Two things I decided without asking, tell me if either is wrong: reactions are in (batched — one row per thing reacted to, “3 people reacted to your reply”), and the section caps at 8 rows with a “N more updates waiting for you” tail rather than running long.

What the notification store does and does not know

The audit that briefed this lane is a month stale in the one way that mattered most. Correcting it here because the next person will read the audit and assume less.

  • It knows more than NOTIFICATIONS-AUDIT.md says. That audit (2026-06-27) records the bell as connection-request/accept only. Since 2026-07-12 it also carries forum.reply_to_topic, forum.reply_to_reply, forum.mention and reaction.on_post — written by lg-shared/notify-bridge.php, with an actor, a coalesced actor_count (“and 2 others”), and a stamped canonical deep link. That last one is why this section did not need a query layer over forums.*: the links already exist.
  • It still does not know about DMs, on purpose. profile-app deliberately does not ring the bell for a new message (“no double-notify”), so unread DMs are read separately from message_recipients.unread_count and merged in.
  • There is no URL that opens a DM thread. The messages surface is a modal opened from the header button; social-modals.js reads no query param and no hash. So a DM row points at the sender’s profile instead of inventing a link that lands nowhere. A ?dm=<thread-uuid> trigger would be a small change and would follow the pattern #search and ?compose=1 already set — but it is a Hub change, not a digest change, so it is flagged rather than done here.
  • WordPress cannot read the store. The WP pool runs as looth-dev, which has zero grants on the profile_app database — the same reason the bridge POSTs over loopback rather than writing PG. The send-time reader is therefore a loopback endpoint, mirroring the existing writer.
  • The per-recipient mechanism works. The thread-follow spec listed FluentCRM per-subscriber rendering as an open feasibility question. It is answered: a custom smart code registered through FluentCrmApi('extender')->addSmartCode() substitutes per subscriber at send, and a raw_html campaign — which is what the digest sends — bypasses FluentCRM’s per-campaign body cache, so each recipient gets their own render from one campaign. Proven on this box against three real subscribers.
