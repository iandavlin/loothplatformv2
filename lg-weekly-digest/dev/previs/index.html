4. “Don't tell me twice” — the one that needs your call

The rule you asked about: skip anything we already emailed at the time rows → no email

You asked for three suppression rules. Two are built and shown above. This is the third, and it is the one I am NOT recommending we build yet — because of what these two pictures show, not because it is hard.

The trap is that it interacts with your “empty means send nothing” ruling. Skipping already-emailed items sounds like it makes an email shorter. It doesn't always. If the skipped rows were all a member had, the email doesn't shrink — it never arrives.

And right now that is not the unlucky case, it is every case. Measured on live today: 258 members get a digest, 7 of them have a discussion item, and all 7 have nothing else. Discussion people and connection people are, for the moment, two separate groups. So this rule would currently turn 7 emails into 7 silences and shorten none.

What I recommend: don't build it yet — but it is no longer safe to call it closed, and I had that wrong. I told the discussion-follow lane this rule could never matter. That was wrong: the digest names “someone replied to your discussion”, and the same reply can also be a per-event email to the same person. One event, two channels.

Nothing to decide today. Today it is 7 members and 5 emails a fortnight. What changes it is the ✉ switch on the discussion toggles — the moment a member can tick “email me” on a discussion they started, this stops being theoretical, and that switch is being built now in the other lane.

So the one thing I would ask you to note, not answer: if we do build it, it needs a floor — never suppress a member's last row. Suppression should be allowed to shorten an email. It should not be allowed to cancel one, because a cancelled email looks exactly like a quiet week and nobody can tell the difference afterwards.

Worth knowing before this sends: “empty sends nothing” suppresses the whole email, so 1,383 of 1,663 subscribed members receive nothing at all this week — including Upcoming Events, the new videos and loothprint, which have nothing to do with anyone's to-do list. That is what the ruling says and it is built that way; flagging it because it changes what the weekly digest is, not just who it greets.

The stale counts are small today: 237 members have anything outstanding, 224 of them have exactly one, and the largest anyone holds is three — so “You have 1 connection request waiting” is the line most people will actually see. Nobody will see 6 this week.
