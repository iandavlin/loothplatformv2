Weekly digest — what gets left out of the email

Three suppression rules, each shown as the email it produces. Real renderer, real live rows.

“Do not duplicate notifications in the email that have already been read — either read in one of those emails, or already read on the website.”

That is three different problems, not one. Two are solved; the third is the only thing here that needs a decision from you. Each rule below is shown as the email without it beside the email with it.

Rule 1 · shipped

Already read on the website

Anything the member already looked at in the bell — or already actioned, for a connection — never enters the email. Live: 24 of 152 items removed.

Rule 2 · do not build

Already sent in a per-event email

Connections, messages and reactions have no email sender at all, so 87% of the digest cannot overlap by construction. Forums can — and forum items are 1 of 128 this week.

Rule 3b · your call

Already sent in a previous digest

A fixed 7-day window assumes last week's email arrived. On 2026-06-01 it did not, for six real members — and what they missed was lost silently.

Rule 3b — the one that needs your call

Grace Da Maren · 6 outstanding items, spread across two weeks 2 rows → 6 rows

This one is the reverse of the others: rows appear. The email counts back a fixed 7 days, which quietly assumes last week's email arrived. On 2026-06-01 it did not — one bad sender signature, six real members, all six still marked failed eight weeks later and never re-sent. Their week fell outside the next window and was never mentioned again. The fix is to count back to the last digest that member actually received, so a failed send heals itself the following week.

Not shown, because it has no picture yet: nothing marks a notification read when someone clicks a link in the email — only the bell does that. So an email-only reader keeps seeing the same items. The obvious fix, a link that clears on click, is deliberately not built: mail scanners follow every link with no human involved, and on this platform's own click data 7–10% of apparent clickers are machines hitting 10–20 links in four seconds. That would wipe a member's recap before they opened it, and it would look exactly like the feature working.
