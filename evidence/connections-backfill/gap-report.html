The connections gap

Were the BuddyBoss friendships backfilled into the new app at the June cutover? Mostly yes — but a specific group was left behind.

Yes, the backfill ran, and it mostly worked — 10,609 of the 11,363 legacy friendships are present in the new app, carrying their original dates all the way back to June 2023. Ian's hypothesis that nothing came across is not what happened.

But 750 friendships were dropped and never re-imported. The cause is not random: the backfill only imported friendships between people who already had a row in wp_user_bridge when it ran. 241 members were added to that bridge after the backfill — most of them on 2026-06-11, nine days later — and 748 of the 750 missing friendships involve one of those late-added members. Only 2 rows are unexplained by that single cause.

The script that did the import, tools/backfill-bb-connections.sh, predicted this in its own header: "RE-RUN AT CUTOVER … rows skipped today are users not yet in wp_user_bridge (unprovisioned) … the ON CONFLICT guard makes re-runs safe." That re-run never happened.

What members will actually see

391 of the 746 rows are pending requests — for Ian, 52 of his 135. Restoring them makes old, unanswered connection requests appear in members' pending UI, dated back to when they were originally sent. A member who opens the site tomorrow may find requests waiting that they have never seen before. That is a real product decision, not a technical detail, and it should be made deliberately.

No notifications and no email will be sent. Verified on live: public.connections carries exactly one trigger, connections_touch, which is BEFORE UPDATE only. There is no INSERT trigger and no rule, and notifications are written by the application layer, not the database. A direct SQL insert mints nothing. The rows simply appear in the UI.

If that is not wanted, the clean split is to restore the 355 accepted rows now and hold the 391 pending ones — the accepted rows are the ones members lost and would expect back, and they carry no call to action.

What is deliberately excluded

The full gap is 750 rows. 746 are restored; 4 are held back on purpose, all of them pending requests:

  • 2 involve a suspended account (WP 1535, patreon_186249137, hide_sitewide=1) — one of them Ian's own. Restoring a pending request to a sitewide-hidden account has no upside.
  • 2 are not explained by the cohort (both to Magnús Gunnar, WP 408). Both endpoints were bridged at cutover, so the backfill should have imported them. The most likely explanation is that it did, and the member later declined them — Connections::decline() performs a hard DELETE rather than setting a declined status, so a declined request and a never-imported one are indistinguishable in the data. Restoring them could resurrect something a member deliberately dismissed.

Both sets are enumerated in excluded-4-rows.tsv. Adding them back is a one-line change if Ian disagrees.

What the fix does

Inserts the 746 rows using exactly the original migration's conventions — is_confirmed=1→accepted, initiator becomes requester, and the original date_created is preserved as both created_at and updated_at, so restored connections keep their true age.

It differs from the original script in one deliberate way. The original relied only on ON CONFLICT (requester_uuid, addressee_uuid), which is directional and cannot see an existing reverse row — which is why live already carries 10 duplicated bidirectional rows. A naive re-run would have added 11 more. The delivered script adds a reverse-existence guard, so it adds none.

Verified against a local replica loaded from this same live snapshot: the insert adds exactly the expected rows, a second run adds 0, and the rollback restores the table to a state that is byte-identical (matching MD5) to the live snapshot it started from.
