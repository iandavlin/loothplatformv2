The follow modal, rethought

You opened it and said it was confusing, and that you could not tell which discussion you were acting on. Both are layout problems, not wording problems — so here are two layouts rather than a reworded version of the one you rejected.

“This modal is a bit confusing. Can we get the title of the discussion we are following?” — 30 July

What was wrong with the one you saw

What you sawWhy it read badly
“Follow this discussion” large and bold at the topThe least useful text in the box was its headline. You already know you tapped follow.
“Spanish Heel failure” small and grey underneathThe one thing you were looking for was styled like a caption, so it read as a footnote to the header.
Two switches, then a bordered button, evenly stackedThree unrelated-looking rows. Nothing said which controls belong together.

Two ways to fix it

ARecommended

Title on top, notifications grouped

The discussion names the box. The two notification bits become one visual group; Save sits apart.

NotificationsA bell row for new replies
EmailsEmail me about new replies

Why this one. It is the smallest change that fixes both complaints, and it keeps every control exactly where you already found it. Nothing moves except emphasis.

B

One decision, then the detail

“Following” is the question. Emails and Save are consequences, and the email row dims until you are following.

Also email meInstead of only the bell

Turn following off and the email row dims — it can never read as a live setting that is not.

The trade. Clearer to read, but it makes Notifications the master switch — today the bell and the email are two independent bits, and B would change that. Only pick B if you want them linked.

One thing to know either way

There is no Frequency row in these, and there was none in what you clicked. You picked variant A from a mock that had Instant / Daily / Weekly in it. I left it out deliberately: nothing sends those digests yet, and a cadence control that quietly does nothing is worse than no control. It goes in the moment the sending side exists — say if you would rather see it greyed out in the meantime.
