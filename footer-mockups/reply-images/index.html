More images on a discussion reply

The headline live defect

This is not only a feature request. Images that members already uploaded are already being hidden from them.

5,118published replies
506carry at least one photo
231carry more than one — and show only the first
11most photos on a single reply

The upload path has never had a cap. Members have been attaching 2, 3, 4, 5 photos to replies for years and the forum has been storing every one of them. The hub replies list then renders exactly one and drops the rest on the floor, with no “+3 more”, no indication anything is missing. 231 replies are in that state right now.

So “allow more images on a reply” is mostly a render change, not an upload change.

The one number the hub gets wrong. The capped image is fetched at w=800 (lg_cover_src() default) into a box that CSS limits to max-height:240px, with no srcset and no width/height. Measured on real photos: 79 KB at w=800 vs 11.6 KB at w=240. The hub is over-fetching about 7× per reply image and taking a layout shift for it.

Why the display cap matters more than the upload cap: with it, a reply stub is never taller than two grid rows and a page of replies never fetches more than six tiles per reply — whatever number you allow people to attach. It decouples “how many may I post” from “what does it cost to scroll”.

The honest cost of a square grid: tiles are object-fit:cover, so a portrait photo loses its top and bottom in the thumbnail. Tapping opens the whole image. The alternative — letting tiles keep their natural aspect — gives ragged rows, which is worse in a list of five replies. Worth a look at the all-portrait frame before signing off; that is where it bites.

One thing this deck caught by measuring rather than reasoning: the first cut declared a flat sizes="33vw" for every layout. The browser probe showed the 2-up and 4-up tiles — which are half-width, not thirds — pulling the w=800 candidate: a 79 KB fetch for a 160 px tile, the exact defect this deck accuses today's code of. sizes now tracks the span rules. Worth remembering when this gets built: the attribute is not decorative.

The number

One recommendation, one alternative.

Recommended: 10 attached, 6 displayed, “+N” for the rest A

Why 10. The most photos anyone has ever put on a real reply is 5 — twice over covers every reply in the forum's history with room to spare. It is a comment, not an album: the topic itself is where a big gallery belongs. And 10 is the largest number that still survives the upload flow as it exists — photos go up one tap at a time (no multiple on any file input), so 20 means twenty separate picks.

Why not higher. Nothing in the render breaks above 10 once display is capped at six — the reasons are upstream: per-file limit is 5 MB, so 20 photos is up to 100 MB uploaded from a phone on cellular for one comment, and the moderation surface grows with no ceiling.

Why not lower. 6 would also be defensible and is cheaper — see below.

Alternative: 6 attached, all 6 displayed, no “+N” at all B

Simpler: no truncation affordance to design, no “+N” tap target, every attached photo visible. 6 still covers 100% of replies ever posted (max is 5). And it is close to free — a previous lane already built and verified this exact cap: render-to-6, the server edit cap, and the client guard are sitting on branch reply-images-6, unmerged. Choosing 6 is mostly a merge; choosing 10 adds the “+N” overflow tile on top of it.

Either way, three things should ship regardless of the number:

  1. Reply images move onto the same srcset / sizes / width&height contract the single-topic page already uses. Today's single image over-fetches ~7× and shifts layout — that is a standing craft-gate violation, independent of counts.
  2. A cap that is enforced server-side on create, not only in the composer. There is no server count check anywhere today, which is exactly why 10- and 11-image replies exist.
  3. multiple on the file input. Without it the cap is theatre: no file input in any composer has it, so “ten photos” means ten separate trips through the picker. It is a one-attribute change and it is the difference between the number being usable and being a number.

Coordination — what this needs from composer-p3

The composer is being rewritten right now. Nothing here should be built against the code that exists today.

Per docs/atlas/COMPOSER-V2-P3-INVENTORY.md, phase 3 repoints entry points E1–E6 at openComposerSheet() and deletes the five native create paths (W1–W5). That is good news for this lane, because the count work lands almost entirely on the render side, and what little lands on the composer lands on one composer instead of three.

Three asks, all small, and none of them a fork:

  1. A cap constant on the seam. openComposerSheet() should accept — or expose — a max-attachment number, and lgcUploadPhoto() should refuse past it with the same error affordance it already uses for a failed upload. Today there is no count check anywhere in that function.
  2. A count read-out. The strip needs “6 of 10” so the cap is visible before it is hit, rather than a rejection at the eleventh tap.
  3. The multiple attribute on #lgc-file, with lgcUploadPhoto() called per file. The per-file machinery to support this is already there — progress, retry, removal, supersede guard, optimistic tile — it is only ever handed files[0].

What this lane does not need from p3: nothing on the render path, nothing on the edit path (edits already PUT the owned mirror endpoint), and no new upload endpoint. If p3 lands its seam as inventoried, the composer half of this is three small changes inside one function.

What does not break

Checked rather than assumed.

  • Weekly digest / email. safe The forum section renders at most one 240px thumbnail per item, sourced from the featured image or the first <img> in post_content (templates/sections/forum.php:16-21). Reply photos are bp_media attachments and are not in post_content, so they never reach the email at all. Structurally immune to the count.
  • Single-topic page. safe Already uncapped and already correct — it is the reference implementation, not a risk.
  • Feed card teaser. safe Deliberately takes one image (_feed.php:896) and defers it behind “Show image”. Should stay at one — the card is a teaser.
  • Upload UX at higher counts. already built Composer v2's lgcUploadPhoto() already does per-file progress, per-file failure with a ↻ retry, per-file removal, an optimistic tile before the first byte leaves, and a supersede guard for re-tries. The thing that would have needed building for 20 photos exists. The legacy lrs composer does not have it — which is the coordination note below.
