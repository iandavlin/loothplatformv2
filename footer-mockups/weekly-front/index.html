The weekly email, on the front page, for logged-out visitors

“Surface the most-recent weekly email on the FRONT PAGE for logged-out visitors.” — your ask, 30 July. Backlog item 8; nobody had picked it up until now.

Two ways to do it, both drawn below in the real front page — these are not pictures, they are the live front-page stylesheet rendering real cards from a real issue, so what you see is what the page would do. Each is shown at desktop and phone width, in light and dark.

Ruled and built, 15 August. You said “build it and let me see it on dev2”, so Option A below is now built — with the placement and the padlocked member-only cards recommended here. This page stays up as the record of what was decided and why. Option B is not built and is one word away if you would rather have it.

The short version: Option A shows the issue’s contents as front-page cards. Option B shows the actual email in a window. I recommend A, and the reason is in the dark-mode and phone panels rather than in an argument.

First — what a logged-out visitor gets today

Nothing, essentially. The front page carries one secondary text button, tucked in the welcome video’s copy: “Weekly email”. There is no issue, no date, no sense that anything goes out weekly at all.

Option A — “This week’s issue”, as front-page cards   recommended

A newsletter masthead — the email’s own brown and gold, with the date it went out — over the issue’s actual contents, rendered as the same cards the rest of the front page already uses. Member-only items keep their padlock, which is the point: a stranger sees what the week held and what membership would open. Underneath, one free ask: get it every Monday, no account needed.

The From the forum strip at the bottom of the block is there because the real issues carry one — the current live issue has two discussions in it. They are drawn with the front page’s discussion card rather than its article card, because that is genuinely what they are and where they live.

Why the dark panel matters here. You said on 14 August that dark mode for logged-out people needs love — “a ton of instructions and fields not ready for primetime in dark for logged out”. This block gets dark right for free, because it is built from the front page’s own colour tokens: when the page goes dark, the block goes dark with it. Three things that panel is showing you, and none of them are fixable by tidying.

1. It cannot go dark. An email has to carry its colours baked in, because inboxes strip stylesheets — so the issue is permanently cream-and-brown. On a dark front page it stays a lit panel in a dark room. Nothing in the frame can change that; it is a property of being an email.

2. On a phone you get the top of it and little else. The window is about 520px tall and the logo alone takes most of that, so a visitor sees a masthead and the first heading. The issue is a long document; the front page can only lend it a letterbox.

3. This is the framing you have already turned down. Back on 30 July, looking at this same treatment on the sign-up page: “the email is now in a container where it floats left and right to see the whole thing without having the text cut off. This sucks.” It is still switched off on the live site for that reason. Putting it on the front page would be shipping it to more people, not fewer.

One thing I found on the way, which you will want fixed either way

On dev2 the sample-email preview is switched on right now for you to test. Look at what it says at the top of Option B’s panel: “Week of August 15, 2026 · 8 items” — while the eight items below it are the ones from the issue that went out on 13 July.

WhatDetail
The symptomAn old issue re-rendered today is stamped with today’s date, so the public sample claims to be this week’s issue while showing last month’s.
The causeclass-lg-wd-email-builder.php line 40 sets the date to “now” every time it renders. That is harmless for a real send, because an issue is built and posted the same day — and wrong the moment anything re-renders an old issue, which is exactly what the public sample does, and exactly what this front-page block would do.
Why it mattersThe sign-up page’s pitch is “this is an issue that actually went out”. A wrong date quietly makes that sentence false, and a visitor who subscribes on Saturday and gets nothing until Monday has been told the wrong thing.
SizeSmall. Pass the issue’s own date into the builder instead of reading the clock. It belongs in whichever build follows this decision.

What I need from you

Three questions. Answer as briefly as you like — a word each is plenty — and I will build it.

1. Which one?

My pick Option A — the issue’s contents as front-page cards. It shows the same week’s content from the same source, it works on a phone and in dark, and it does not re-ship the framing you turned down in July.

Option B is the honest alternative and it is drawn fairly: if seeing the actual email is the whole point for you, say so and I will build B instead.

2. Where should it sit?

My pick Directly under the welcome video, above the featured member — which is where it is drawn in every panel above. High enough to be the shop window you described, without pushing the welcome video off the top.

The alternative is lower down, under the featured member, if you would rather the person came first.

3. Should member-only items show at all?

My pick Yes, with their padlock — three of the seven cards in the panels above are member-only, and seeing them locked is the clearest argument for joining that the page can make. No titles are hidden and no member content leaks; the padlock and the tier badge are already how the front page handles this everywhere else.

On the current live issue the mix is starker still: four of its five videos are member-only (one Pro, three Lite). Public-only would leave that section showing a single video.

The alternative is public items only, which is safer but makes a thin, oddly short issue.
