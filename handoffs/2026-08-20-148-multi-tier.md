## What was actually wrong (step 1, the verify-first trace)

**The grant was a constant — but `looth3`, not `looth2`, and it sits on the path
that is currently switched OFF.** Two Stripe grant paths exist:

- **What runs on dev2 today** — Slim `ReturnHandler` → `tierForPrice($priceId)` →
  entitlement → `Sync::customer` → `RoleSourceWriter` → `Arbiter` → WP role.
  **Already tier-correct.** LITE→looth2, PRO→looth3.
- **`StripeLifecycle`**, the designated production replacement (flag
  `lgms_stripe_lifecycle`, OFF everywhere) — `applyConfirmed()` reads `$priceId`
  at line 397 for the subscription upsert, then **ignores it** and applies
  `self::TIER` at line 426.

**The defect direction is the opposite of the one the issue expected. Nobody was
under-granted: a member buying Looth LITE at $5 was granted PRO.** And it is not
additive — `grantMembershipFromSubscription` revokes by source and re-inserts on
any ref change, so switching the lifecycle on would have **overwritten** the
correctly-resolved looth2 entitlement with looth3.

This was not a mistake. It is the faithful implementation of the 8/08 ruling,
still quoted verbatim in the class docblock. The 8/19 ruling supersedes it, so
the work was retiring that ruling from the three places it was hardcoded: the
grant, `StripePrice::tierProduct()` (the dash could only ever price PRO), and the
WP checkout door (cadence-only).

## What was built

1. **The grant follows the price**, behind `lgms_multi_tier` (option, default
   OFF). An unmapped price falls back to the constant and **names the price in
   the log — never null**, because null reaches `applyOpinion` as a RETRACTION
   (the cancellation shape) and would demote somebody who just paid.
2. **The dash is tier × cadence.** Options are now
   `lgms_stripe_price_<tier>_<cadence>`. The fallback chain to the two legacy
   options is **closed to non-default tiers** — they were written when there was
   one tier, so letting looth2 read them would sell Looth LITE at the Pro price,
   silently, on a box that looked correctly configured. Tiers come from the
   catalogue, so registering a product is the whole of adding a tier. Dash-only,
   no flag.
3. **The WP checkout door names a tier** — validated against configured prices,
   unknown tier refused with 400 rather than falling back. A price id in the body
   is as ignored as it always was.
4. **Gate 76**, 40 assertions, registered in run-all/CRAFT-STANDARD/FLAGS.
5. **`docs/domains/MEMBERSHIP.md`** updated.

## The join page (step 3) — verified, no change needed, but it found a defect

Verified in a real browser as an authenticated admin: **two cards, Looth LITE and
Looth PRO**, straight from the catalogue. `renderTiers` already keys off
`products.length`. Nothing to do.

But the picture showed something else: **the Looth PRO card offers FOUR buttons —

for the same membership, live on dev2 now. `setPrice` never retired the price it
superseded, and `renderTiers` draws one button per active recurring price with no
dedup by cadence.

Fixed — and the first version of my fix was **wrong in a way only the photograph
caught**: it retired the price the *option* pointed at, while dev2's options name

(product, rhythm) recurring set. Safe for members still billing on a retired
price, and I checked rather than assumed: `lg_ms_lookup_active_sub` joins `prices`
with **no active filter**, and both `tierForPrice` implementations filter on the
**product's** active flag, never the price row's.

## Real-stack proof (added after the first report)

The report above admitted the ON path had only ever run against SQLite. That gap
is now closed. `e2e-stripe-lifecycle-dev2.php` gained a section 7 that runs
through the **real WP bootstrap, the real `lg_membership` MySQL, the real
`IdentityMatcher`, and the real `Arbiter` writing real `wp_capabilities`**.
Flags are injected via `pre_option` filters, so `wp_options` is never written and
the box's real flag state is untouched.

**38 passed, 0 failed.** The leg that makes the rest mean anything is the first
one: **with the flag OFF, a LITE subscription really is granted `looth3` on
dev2.** The defect demonstrated on the real stack, not inferred from a reading.
Then the same event under the flag grants `looth2`, revokes the `looth3`
entitlement, and leaves real `wp_capabilities` carrying `looth2` with `looth3`
**gone** — corrected, not merely added to. An unmapped price falls back to the
constant rather than demoting a payer.

Prices are resolved from the catalogue at runtime, not hardcoded — a pinned price
id would rot the first time the dash set a new one and then fail as though the
feature were broken. Scratch fixtures are torn down; I verified zero leaked users
and zero leftover e2e customers afterwards.
